\section{Velocity Vector Visualizations}
\label{app:vectorplots}

Figure~\ref{fig:app_uvw_vectors} shows the full three-dimensional velocity vectors
$(U, V, W)$ of the subsample of 180 stars for which a radial-velocity measurement is
available, plotted at their respective three-dimensional positions within the
reconstruction volume. Figure~\ref{fig:app_pm_vectors} shows the tangential velocities of the full
557-star catalog, drawn as three-dimensional vectors to enable comparison with the full velocity
vectors and the velocity field, even though they are only a projection onto the plane of the sky.
The sky plane mostly follows the $Y$--$Z$ plane, as the Sun lies
at the origin and Taurus covers only a small patch of sky in the $-X$ direction, so the plotted
vectors barely extend in that direction.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.9\linewidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/UVW.png}
        \caption{Full three-dimensional velocity vectors of the complete radial-velocity
        subsample (n=180), shown at their three-dimensional positions. Colorbar clamped at
        $50~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ to account for outliers.}
        \label{fig:app_uvw_vectors}
    \end{subfigure}
    \vspace{1em}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.9\linewidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pm_vectors.png}
        \caption{Proper-motion vectors of the full catalog.}
        \label{fig:app_pm_vectors}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Vector-based illustrations of the two velocity data modalities entering the
        reconstruction: full three-dimensional space velocities of the radial-velocity
        subsample (top) versus proper motions of the full sample, represented as three-dimensional
        vectors (bottom).}

\end{figure}


\section{Density Mask}
\label{app:mass_density}

Figure~\ref{fig:app_mass_density} shows the stellar mass density underlying the density mask: a
Gaussian kernel density estimate of the stellar positions, weighted by the individual stellar masses
and computed with \texttt{scipy.stats.gaussian\_kde} \citep{Virtanen2020} on the reconstruction grid, scaled by the total
mass of the sample to give $\mathrm{M_\odot\,pc^{-3}}$. Masking at any value above zero
restricts every figure of the reconstruction to the regions populated by data with variable extent
depending on the chosen cut.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/mass_density.png}
    \caption{Mass density of the stellar dataset. Brighter regions are denser.}
    \label{fig:app_mass_density}
\end{figure}


\section{Field Derivatives}
\label{app:derivatives}

Figure~\ref{fig:app_derivatives} shows the two derived quantities discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:Derivatives}, both in units of $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,pc^{-1}}$ and restricted to
the masked region. The divergence is signed: positive values mark regions where the field expands,
negative ones where it converges. Of the vorticity only the magnitude $|\nabla\times\vec{v}|$ is
shown, as its direction, the axis of the local rotation, is not constrained well enough at this
resolution to be meaningfully interpretable.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.9\linewidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vfield_div.png}
        \caption{Divergence $\nabla\cdot\vec{v}$ of the reconstructed field. Positive values
            indicate expansion, negative values convergence.}
        \label{fig:app_divergence}
    \end{subfigure}
    \vspace{1em}
    \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.9\linewidth}
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/vfield_curl.png}
        \caption{Magnitude of the vorticity $|\nabla\times\vec{v}|$ of the reconstructed field.}
        \label{fig:app_curl}
    \end{subfigure}
    \caption{Spatial derivatives of the reconstructed velocity field, computed by central
        differences on the grid: divergence (top) and vorticity magnitude (bottom).}
    \label{fig:app_derivatives}
\end{figure}
